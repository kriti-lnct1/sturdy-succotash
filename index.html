About · परिचय

What is KRITI?

कृति (Kriti) means a creation or a work. KRITI: IKS Tech Challenge invites students to take a classical Indian idea from a primary text, formalise it, build something with it, and test it against a modern benchmark. The aim is work that is authentic, measurable and reproducible.

More informationShow lessPrinciples and how it works
AuthenticityEvery textual claim traced to a primary source.
RigourEvery performance claim backed by measured evidence.
OriginalityWork is created during the challenge; borrowed material is declared.
IntegrityHonest reporting, including negative results.
RespectFor traditions, texts, communities and each other.
  1. ChoosePick one problem from the four branch tracks. Confirm your choice once your team is confirmed.
  2. BuildBuild with mentor checkpoints and a source-verification desk.
  3. Validate and presentCompare with a modern baseline, then demo to the Jury.

What you take away

Research-style problems

Problems that need a source, a method and a measurable result, not just a demo.

Mentors and expert Jury

Faculty, domain experts and IKS scholars review your approach at three checkpoints.

Recognition

Track awards, special awards and certificates for every valid submission.

Portfolio-ready output

A documented repository, deck and video that you can show to recruiters and admissions panels.

Four branch tracks, twelve problems

CSE Computer Science, IT & AI Track Ganaka

CSE-01Sanskrit SetuIntelligent Sanskrit Knowledge Explorer

Problem statement

Ancient Indian knowledge is preserved in Sanskrit manuscripts, inscriptions and texts, but students often face difficulties understanding unfamiliar vocabulary, finding relevant passages and connecting traditional concepts with modern applications.

The task

Develop an interactive digital platform that allows users to explore selected Sanskrit passages through word meanings, grammatical analysis, translations and contextual explanations. The platform should help users discover how a selected concept relates to a modern field such as mathematics, architecture, astronomy or environmental conservation.

IKS connection

Sanskrit language, traditional textual scholarship and knowledge transmission.

Demonstration expectation

A working interface that accepts a selected passage, highlights words, displays meanings and explains one carefully researched traditional concept with references.

Possible implementation

NLP, a curated Sanskrit glossary, searchable texts, rule-based processing or AI-assisted explanation with source verification.

CSE-02KalaGanakaInteractive Indian Timekeeping and Astronomy Lab

Problem statement

Traditional Indian systems of timekeeping and astronomical observation are difficult for many learners to visualise and compare with modern scientific measurements.

The task

Create an educational simulation that demonstrates a selected traditional timekeeping method or astronomical instrument, explains its underlying principle and allows users to change inputs and observe results. Where historical calculations are used, clearly distinguish the original method from modern scientific comparisons.

IKS connection

Indian mathematical astronomy, calendrical traditions and historical observational instruments.

Demonstration expectation

An interactive simulation or digital instrument with adjustable parameters, visual outputs and a documented comparison against a suitable modern reference.

Possible implementation

JavaScript, Python, animation, mathematical modelling or a 3D educational interface.

CSE-03IntermediateMeruCodeTurn Pingala’s patterns into a digital encoding system

Problem statement

Create a program inspired by the binary patterns and combinatorial arrangements described in Pingala’s Chhandas-Shastra. The application should generate patterns and use them to encode and decode a short text or binary sequence.

The challenge

Participants must define their encoding scheme, reconstruct the original input correctly and compare storage size and execution time with a simple baseline. They must demonstrate whether their approach actually compresses the selected data or merely encodes it.

Final deliverable

An interactive pattern generator and encoder/decoder with correctness tests and comparison results.

IKS connection

Pingala’s prosody and Meru Prastara. The modern encoding method must be identified as the team’s implementation, not claimed as a historical compression system.

EEE Electrical & Electronics Track Vidyut

EEE-01IntermediateVedicBitDesign and test a digital multiplier

Problem statement

Implement a digital multiplier inspired by the Urdhva Tiryagbhyam multiplication method and compare it with a conventional binary multiplication implementation.

The challenge

Both implementations must return correct results for the same inputs. Participants must compare operation counts or simulated logic delay and explain whether their design offers an advantage under the chosen conditions.

Final deliverable

A digital logic simulation or interactive software demo with test cases and comparison graphs.

IKS connection

A documented Indian mathematical technique translated into a modern digital design. An FPGA or physical circuit is optional.

EEE-02Easy–IntermediateSuryaSwitchBuild an intelligent energy controller

Problem statement

Develop a controller that uses light intensity and operating conditions to manage a small solar-powered lighting system efficiently.

The challenge

Compare a fixed operating schedule with an adaptive controller. Measure energy consumed, lighting availability and system response under changing light conditions. Include a manual override or fallback mode.

Final deliverable

A low-voltage prototype or circuit simulation with a dashboard showing energy and operating-state comparisons.

IKS connection

Indian astronomical traditions provide a context for systematic solar observation; the electronic controller is a modern engineering application.

EEE-03MicroHarvestGet useful energy from intermittent motion

Problem

Intermittent motion produces small amounts of electrical energy. Design a system that converts, stores and uses that energy for a low-power task.

The challenge

The energy source is inconsistent; teams must minimise conversion losses and decide when enough stored energy is available to perform the task.

Demonstration

Use a safe, low-voltage source and measure input energy, stored energy and successful task cycles.

IKS direction

Treat this as a modern energy-conversion challenge unless a well-supported traditional connection is established.

MECH Mechanical Track Yantra

MECH-01IntermediateThermalPathImprove a traditional furnace’s heat-flow design

Problem statement

Create a simplified thermal model inspired by historical Indian metalworking or zinc-distillation furnace designs, and investigate how insulation and geometry affect heat loss.

The challenge

Compare two or three designs under identical simulated operating conditions. Identify where heat is lost, estimate the effect of insulation changes and explain the assumptions behind the model.

Final deliverable

A CAD-based thermal representation or computational simulation with temperature profiles and a comparative efficiency estimate.

IKS connection

Historical metallurgical practices, such as the zinc-distillation traditions of Zawar. Use a simulation or low-temperature demonstration; no high-temperature furnace is required.

MECH-02HeatTrapCooling without a fan

Problem

Design a passive cooling enclosure for a small electronic device operating under a fixed heat load.

The challenge

Keep temperature below a target using limited surface area, material and enclosure volume.

Proof

Compare temperature-versus-time curves for different geometries.

IKS direction

Investigate documented traditional ventilation and thermal-management design principles.

MECH-03WearWisePredict component wear from test data

Problem

Build a simple test-and-analysis system that estimates how friction conditions affect the wear of a replaceable component.

The challenge

Develop a model from limited measurements and test whether it predicts results from a new trial.

Proof

Compare predicted and measured wear, report uncertainty and identify where the model fails.

IKS direction

Investigate historical metalworking and tool-maintenance practices as context; the prediction method is the modern contribution.

CIV Civil Track Nirmana

CIV-01Vastushilpa AISmart structural design

Category

Traditional Architecture & Modern Engineering

Problem statement

Design a generative AI tool or software plug-in that translates traditional Vastu Shastra and Shilpa Shastra principles (like Ayadi calculations, spatial symmetry and solar alignment) into optimised modern AutoCAD/Revit building plans. The solution must ensure the design fulfils modern structural safety standards (IS 456) and achieves maximum energy efficiency.

Expected input

Plot dimensions, geographical location, climate data and building type.

Expected output

2D/3D BIM layout optimised for natural lighting and ventilation, with a Vastu-compliance score and structural safety validation.

CIV-02JalarangamAncient water management systems

Category

Hydro-geology & Smart Water Storage

Problem statement

Develop an IoT-based smart network and predictive GIS model inspired by ancient Indian water harvesting systems (like the stepwells of Gujarat, Ahar-Pyne of Bihar or Eri cascades of Tamil Nadu). The system should optimise urban runoff collection, prevent local flooding and automate groundwater recharge management in modern tier-2 and tier-3 cities.

Expected input

Rainfall data, local topography maps (GIS), urban drainage layouts and soil permeability data.

Expected output

An automated drainage diversion algorithm, a real-time water storage monitoring dashboard and a lifecycle carbon-reduction report.

CIV-03Setu-VijnanaResilient modular infrastructure

Category

Bridge Engineering & Disaster Resilience

Problem statement

Design an algorithmic optimisation tool for building modular, fast-assembly bridges in disaster-prone or hilly terrains, adopting structural interlocking and load-distribution principles found in ancient Indian timber and stone bridges (like the living root bridges or suspension structures). The design must withstand high seismic activity.

Rules · नियम

Rules at a glance

More informationShow lessAll six rules

The full Official Rulebook is shared with shortlisted teams. These are the essentials.

Fresh-start rule

All substantive work is done during the Build Period. You may read sources and set up your environment beforehand, but not write solution code.

AI tools

Permitted as an aid, with disclosure. Every team member must be able to explain and reproduce their work, and every AI-generated citation must be verified.

Cite everything

Textual claims need exact references. Performance claims need measured data against a stated baseline.

Honest results

Fabricated data or citations lead to disqualification. Negative and inconclusive results are welcome when reported honestly.

Safety first

Lab work only under supervision with protective equipment. No molten-metal work at the venue.

Respectful conduct

Zero tolerance for harassment or discrimination. Traditions and texts are treated with respect, and unverifiable claims are not promoted.

Judging · मूल्यांकन

How teams are evaluated

More informationShow lessMarking rubric

Each Jury member scores the demonstration round independently out of 100 marks.

IKS research and authenticity20

Accuracy of the traditional knowledge, credible sources and explanation of its relevance.

Problem understanding15

Clarity of the real-world problem, users affected and assumptions.

Innovation and solution design20

Originality, thoughtful adaptation and suitability of the proposed solution.

Working demonstration25

A functional prototype, simulation, experiment or interactive model with observable results.

Feasibility and impact10

Practicality, limitations, sustainability and potential usefulness.

Presentation and Q&A10

Clear explanation, teamwork and ability to defend design decisions.

A demonstration should not require a fully engineered commercial product.

FAQ · जिज्ञासा

Frequently asked questions

More informationShow lessAll questions, search and categories
What is KRITI?
KRITI (कृति) is the IKS Tech Challenge organised by Avadhan Society, LNCT Bhopal, on 30 October 2026. Teams take an idea from a classical Indian text, formalise it, build something with it and test it against a modern benchmark.
Is this a hackathon?
It is hackathon-style: teams build a working solution to a chosen problem. The difference is the emphasis on authentic sources and measured validation, not only a demo.
What does IKS mean?
Indian Knowledge Systems: the classical body of Indian knowledge in mathematics, astronomy, metallurgy, architecture, linguistics and more.
Who can take part?
Currently enrolled students of any branch with a valid college ID card. Students from other institutions should write to us before registering so we can confirm eligibility.
Can first-year students take part?
Yes. Students of every year are welcome, and there is a Best Beginner Team award for teams of first and second-year students.
Can I participate alone?
No. Teams must have two or three members.
Can all my team members be from one branch?
Yes. Members may all be from one branch or from different branches. One member acts as the Source Person.
Can I be in more than one team?
No. Every participant belongs to exactly one team.
Can I change a team member after registering?
Write to the Organisers as early as possible. The Rulebook shared with shortlisted teams states the rules on team changes.
Do I need to know Sanskrit?
Not necessarily. The Source Person should be able to read the relevant text or work from a verified translation. A source-verification desk is available during the event for advice.
How do I register?
Fill the Google Form between 17 and 28 October 2026. Submit one form per team with your member details and a short abstract.
What should the abstract contain?
Keep it short: the classical idea or text you want to work with, the modern problem it connects to, and how you plan to approach it.
Is there a registration fee?
Yes. The registration fee is ₹199 per team, not per member. The registration form explains how to pay.
When will I know if my team is confirmed?
Abstracts are screened after registration closes on 28 October. Confirmed teams receive their Team ID and the Rulebook by email on 29 October.
When will I see the problem statements?
They are already published on this page. Open Tracks and tap any problem to read the full statement.
Can I use AI tools?
Yes, as an aid. You must disclose the tools you used, verify every citation they produce, and be able to explain and reproduce your work.
Can I start building before the event?
No. You can read sources, study your Problem Pack and set up your environment, but solution work begins with the Build Period.
Where is the venue and what should I carry?
The event is in Bhopal and the exact venue is shared with confirmed teams. Carry your college ID, signed Participant Undertaking, laptop, charger and offline copies of your Problem Pack. See the full list under Who can take part.
Will I get a certificate?
Every participant receives a certificate. Prize money of ₹10,000 and trophies or medals are given at the valedictory.
What if my experiment or model does not work?
Report it honestly. Negative results are credited, and there is a dedicated award for rigorous reporting of them.
What happens if citations or data are copied or fabricated?
Fabricated data or citations lead to disqualification. Borrowed material is allowed only when declared.
Who do I contact with questions?
See the Contact section below. Questions about a specific problem must go through the official Clarification Form so that every team receives the same answer.
